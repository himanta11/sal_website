90-Day Master Study Plan to Crack ADRE Grade 3 in First Attempt

By Scholar Academy Academic Team Published November 2026 6 Min Read Dibrugarh & Tezpur, Assam

Cracking the Assam Direct Recruitment Examination (ADRE) Grade 3 on your very first attempt does not require studying 14 hours a day—it requires a structured, syllabus-focused roadmap. With lakhs of aspirants competing for state government vacancies across Assam, random reading will only waste valuable time.

At Scholar Academy of Learning (Dibrugarh & Tezpur), we have mentored hundreds of successful state-level exam qualifiers. Based on previous ADRE question paper trends, we have designed a practical 90-Day Master Study Plan divided into three clear phases: Concept Building, Practice & Speed, and Mock Test Mastery.

Understanding the ADRE Grade 3 Exam Pattern First

Before jumping into the timetable, keep the exact exam structure in front of your study desk so every hour you spend aligns with the marking scheme:

Subject / Section Key Focus Areas Weightage Priority
Social Studies & General Knowledge Assam History, Geography, Culture, Polity, National Parks, Govt. Schemes Very High
General Mathematics Simplification, Percentage, Ratio, Profit & Loss, Time & Work, Mensuration High
Logical Reasoning & Mental Ability Number Series, Coding-Decoding, Blood Relations, Syllogism, Seating Arrangement High
General English Reading Comprehension, Synonyms/Antonyms, Error Spotting, Prepositions, Idioms Medium-High
General Science & Current Affairs Everyday Science (Class 8–10 NCERT), Last 6–8 Months Assam & National Events Medium
Golden Rule: Never ignore Assam-specific General Knowledge and Basic Mathematics. Together, these two sections decide the final merit list cut-off.

Ideal Daily Study Timetable (6 Hours Focused Routine)

Whether you are a full-time aspirant or a college student, stick to this daily 6-hour split for the next 90 days:

Time Slot Duration Study Activity
Morning (7:00 AM – 9:00 AM) 2 Hours General Knowledge & Assam History/Geography (Fresh mind for memory retention)
Late Morning (10:30 AM – 12:30 PM) 2 Hours Quantitative Aptitude (Maths) – Concept + 30 practice questions
Afternoon (4:00 PM – 5:00 PM) 1 Hour General English & Current Affairs (Daily editorial vocab + Assam news notes)
Evening (7:30 PM – 8:30 PM) 1 Hour Logical Reasoning + Previous Day Revision

Phase 1: Days 1 to 30 — Foundation & Syllabus Coverage

The goal of the first 30 days is simple: complete 80% of the static syllabus without worrying about speed.

  • Assam GK & Social Studies: Start with the Ahom Kingdom, Koch Dynasty, Assam Freedom Movement, Geography of Assam (rivers, hills, national parks), and Indian Polity basics (Fundamental Rights, Articles, Amendments).
  • Mathematics: Clear your basics from Class 6 to 10 NCERT. Master tables up to 20, squares up to 30, and cubes up to 15. Focus on Percentage, Ratio & Proportion, Average, and Simplification first.
  • English: Revise basic grammar rules (Tenses, Subject-Verb Agreement, Prepositions, Articles) and learn 10 new synonyms/antonyms daily.
  • Reasoning: Practice Coding-Decoding, Analogy, Direction Sense, and Blood Relations.
  • Weekend Task (Every Sunday): Solve one Previous Year Question (PYQ) paper on PYQscore to understand the exact difficulty level of questions asked by SLRC.

Phase 2: Days 31 to 60 — Speed Building & PYQ Practice

Once your fundamentals are clear, shift from passive reading to active question-solving.

  • Topic-Wise Timed Practice: Solve 40–50 mixed questions daily under a strict timer. In ADRE Grade 3, accuracy matters just as much as speed because of negative marking.
  • Assam Current Affairs & Schemes: Compile notes on the last 6 months of Assam Government schemes, awards, sports achievements, and state budget highlights.
  • Advance Maths & Data Interpretation: Move to Time & Work, Speed-Time-Distance, Simple & Compound Interest, Geometry basics, and Bar/Pie Chart DI.
  • Error Notebook: Maintain a dedicated notebook for Maths formulas and GK facts that you repeatedly forget. Revise this notebook every night for 20 minutes.

Phase 3: Days 61 to 90 — Full-Length Mock Tests & Final Revision

The final 30 days are where ranks are made. Stop picking up new books during this phase.

  • Days 61 to 75: Attempt 3 full-length OMR-based mock tests per week. Spend at least 1.5 hours analyzing every test—identify silly mistakes, unattempted easy questions, and weak chapters.
  • Days 76 to 85: Increase frequency to 1 full mock test every alternate day on PYQscore to build exam-hall stamina and time management.
  • Days 86 to 90 (Final 5 Days): Zero new topics. Only revise your short notes, Assam GK one-liners, Maths formula sheets, and static lists (dance forms, festivals, sanctuaries, constitutional articles).

4 Common Mistakes That Cost First-Attempt Selections

Avoid These Critical Pitfalls:

  1. Ignoring Previous Year Papers: Many students study UPSC-level material while skipping actual ADRE pattern questions. Always benchmark your preparation with real PYQs.
  2. Collecting Too Many Books: Reading one standard book three times is far better than reading three books once.
  3. Skipping Mock Test Analysis: Giving a test without reviewing wrong answers repeats the same errors on exam day.
  4. Leaving Maths & Reasoning for the End: Practice quantitative and reasoning questions daily—even 45 minutes a day builds unbeatable confidence.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q1. Is 90 days enough to crack ADRE Grade 3 in the first attempt?

Yes. With 5–6 hours of disciplined daily study, complete syllabus coverage in the first 60 days, and regular mock test analysis in the final 30 days, 90 days is more than enough to clear ADRE Grade 3.

Q2. Which subject carries the highest weightage in ADRE Grade 3?

Social Studies and General Knowledge (especially Assam History, Geography, Culture, and Polity) along with General Mathematics carry the highest scoring potential in ADRE Grade 3.

Q3. Where can I practice free ADRE Grade 3 previous year question papers online?

You can practice free ADRE previous year question papers and timed mock tests directly on PYQscore (www.pyqscore.in) to track your accuracy and speed.
